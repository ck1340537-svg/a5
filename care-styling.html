Live with it

Caring for and styling crochet bags

Gentle care routines for every fibre and practical ideas for wearing handmade bags from spring markets to winter evenings.

Care basics

Treat it like the handwork it is

Handmade bags are surprisingly durable, but they reward a little gentleness. The golden rule: keep natural fibres like raffia, straw and jute dry, and never put any crochet bag in a washing machine unless the maker says it is safe.

  • Brush away dust and sand with a soft, clean brush.
  • Dab small marks with a barely damp cloth and a drop of mild soap.
  • Let damp bags dry naturally, stuffed with paper, away from radiators and direct sun.
  • Avoid overloading, which stretches stitches and handles.

Styling: warm weather

Summer, made easy

Natural fibres look best with natural fabrics. Pair a raffia or jute bag with linen, cotton and simple leather sandals. Let the texture of the bag be the focal point and keep jewellery minimal.

For holidays, a large tote doubles as a beach bag by day and a market bag by evening. Add a silk scarf tied to the handle for a pop of colour.

Styling: all year

Not just for summer

Cotton crochet in deeper tones such as chocolate, olive, rust and navy works beautifully in cooler months. Try a structured crochet tote with a camel coat and loafers, or a compact crochet bag with knitwear for a cosy, tactile look.

For evenings, a small crochet pouch in fine cotton or a silk blend, perhaps with a chain strap, feels both relaxed and refined.

Repairs

Small fixes that save a bag

  • Loose end: weave it back in with a tapestry needle along the stitches.
  • Pulled loop: gently ease the slack back through neighbouring stitches with a small hook.
  • Stretched base: add a cut-to-size base insert or a sturdy fabric lining.
  • Worn handles: wrap with leather or cotton tape, or ask a maker to replace them.

Always follow the maker’s care instructions where provided.
