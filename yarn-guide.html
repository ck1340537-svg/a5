Materials

A guide to bag yarns and fibres

From palm-leaf raffia to silky cotton, here is how the most common bag materials behave, how they feel and what each is best for.

Why fibre matters

The yarn makes the bag

A crochet bag has to do two jobs: look beautiful and carry weight without sagging. The fibre you choose affects both. Stiffer materials like jute and raffia hold their shape naturally, while softer yarns such as cotton need denser stitches or a lining to stay structured.

Weight matters too. Thicker yarns and cords work up quickly and create bold texture, while fine threads allow intricate patterns and elegant evening bags.

Natural vs. imitation

Is it real raffia?

“Raffia” is often used loosely to describe any straw-like yarn. Genuine raffia comes from the leaves of raffia palms and has slight natural variation in width and colour. Paper and synthetic alternatives are more uniform and sometimes shinier.

  • Look: natural raffia has subtle irregularities and a soft, matte-to-satin sheen.
  • Feel: it is light and slightly flexible, not plasticky.
  • Label: check the fibre content; honest makers will tell you exactly what they used.

Hook size

Smaller hooks with thicker yarn create a tight, firm fabric ideal for bags. The yarn label suggests a starting size.

Ply

The number of strands twisted together. More plies generally mean a stronger, more even yarn.

Tension

How tightly the yarn is held. Consistent tension is the hallmark of an experienced maker.

Hardware

Handles, linings and closures

The finishing pieces turn a crochet fabric into a bag that lasts.

  • Leather handles add strength and a polished, luxurious look.
  • Wooden or bamboo rings suit beach baskets and boho styles.
  • Fabric linings in cotton or linen stop stretching and protect contents.
  • Magnetic snaps or drawstrings keep things secure without spoiling the shape.
Responsible fibres

Choosing thoughtfully

Natural, plant-based fibres such as raffia, jute and cotton are renewable and biodegradable at the end of their life. Look for makers who are open about where their materials come from and who pay fair prices for handwork.

A well-made bag that lasts for many summers is one of the most sustainable choices of all.
